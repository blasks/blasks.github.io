Stephen Blaskowski

Experience

Postdoctoral Researcher (Seattle, Washington) 2025 – present

University of Washington Department of Oceanography | PI: Ginger Armbrust

  • Developed a maximum-likelihood framework for estimating gene prevalence from incomplete genomes, extending prevalence estimates to 52,500 Prochlorococcus gene families, more than four times the number possible with complete genomes alone.
  • Leading research on global expression of the Prochlorococcus pangenome and the role of horizontally-transferred flexible genes in anti-viral defense.
  • Building reproducible, containerized Snakemake pipelines (Docker, Apptainer) for analyzing genomic and metatranscriptomic data.

Graduate Research Associate (Seattle, Washington) 2018 – 2024

University of Washington Department of Oceanography | PI: Ginger Armbrust

  • Developed Barnacle, a sparse tensor decomposition algorithm and open-source Python package, and applied it to ocean metatranscriptomes to reveal simultaneous acclimation to nitrogen and iron scarcity in open ocean cyanobacteria (Science Advances, 2025).
  • Collected in situ data and specimens as a member of a 5-week open ocean expedition to the eastern Equatorial Pacific aboard the RV Thomas G. Thompson.
  • Mentored undergraduate researchers with computational and wet lab projects, including some who presented at the UW Undergraduate Research Symposium.

Research Associate III (Emeryville, California) 2016 – 2018

Zymergen Inc. | Supervisor: Shawn Szyjka

  • Developed novel gene and strain engineering technologies alongside R&D team colleagues.
  • Authored three patents and one publication in coordination with colleagues across departments.
  • Promoted twice in two years from Research Associate I to Research Associate III.

Research Associate (Silver Spring, Maryland) 2014 – 2016

U.S. Military HIV Research Program | PI: Victoria Polonis

  • Adapted manual antibody-response assay to high-throughput automated liquid handling platform.
  • Sustained core laboratory operations, including maintaining cell lines and safeguarding samples.

Undergraduate Researcher (Boulder, Colorado) 2013 – 2014

University of Colorado Department of Molecular, Cellular, and Developmental Biology | PI: Shelley Copley

  • Cloned library of E. coli mutant strains used to investigate directed evolution of a key metabolic pathway.
  • Assisted in the development of a scarless genome editing method for E. coli and Salmonella (Kim et al., BMC Biotechnology, 2014).
